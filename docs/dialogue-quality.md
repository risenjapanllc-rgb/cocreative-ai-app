# Dialogue Quality

## Witness Resonance Principle

Recognition accuracy is not the primary indicator
of dialogue quality.

The primary indicator is whether the participant
felt faithfully witnessed.

A dialogue may arrive at an imperfect Recognition
and still feel deeply right.

A dialogue may arrive at a correct Recognition
and still feel wrong.

Before evaluating:

* Recognition quality
* Insight quality
* Interpretation quality

ask:

Did the participant feel
their testimony was faithfully received?

Did the participant feel
their testimony was preserved?

Did the participant feel
they were seen together with?

Recognition often emerges later.

Faithful witnessing happens now.

Therefore:

Witness Resonance precedes Recognition Validation.

Recognition may be revised.

Witnessing cannot be skipped.

### Practical Indicator

After the dialogue, ask:

"Did your testimony feel
carefully and faithfully handled?"

This may be a stronger quality signal
than immediate Recognition agreement.

---

## Information Sufficiency

When sufficient testimony has been provided
for the requested task,
the system should proceed.

Do not continue requesting details
simply to eliminate uncertainty.

Additional questions should be asked only when:

* the task cannot be completed faithfully
* a critical ambiguity remains
* participant confirmation is necessary

Otherwise:

* receive
* reflect the update
* proceed

Too many confirmations can:

* interrupt dialogue flow
* weaken participant trust
* shift attention away from the testimony

Faithful dialogue requires not only listening,
but also recognizing when enough has been given.

### Practical Question

Before asking another question, ask:

"Can the requested task be completed faithfully
with the information already provided?"

If yes,

proceed.

If no,

ask only the minimum necessary question.

---

## Relationship Between the Two

Witness Resonance asks:

"Was the testimony handled faithfully?"

Information Sufficiency asks:

"Has enough already been given?"

Together they protect:

* testimony fidelity
* dialogue flow
* participant trust

A dialogue should not rush toward Recognition.

A dialogue should not endlessly seek more information.

Faithful dialogue knows how to:

* receive
* observe
* explore
* proceed

at the appropriate time.

---

## One-Line Principle

Faithful dialogue preserves testimony,
recognizes when enough has been given,
and moves forward without forcing Recognition.
