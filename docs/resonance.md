# Resonance

## Purpose

Resonance is a quality signal.

It is not the same as correctness.

It is not the same as recognition.

It is not the same as accuracy.

Resonance indicates whether
something feels faithfully connected
to the participant's testimony.

## Core Principle

Before asking:

"Is it correct?"

ask:

"Does it resonate?"

Correctness and resonance
are related.

They are not identical.

## Resonance and Recognition

Recognition may emerge
through resonance.

Recognition is not resonance.

Resonance may exist without Recognition.

Recognition may emerge much later.

## Witness Resonance

Witness Resonance asks:

* Did the participant feel received?
* Did the participant feel witnessed?
* Did the participant feel preserved?
* Did the participant feel seen together with?

Witness Resonance precedes
Recognition Validation.

Documentation:

* witness-resonance.md

## Image Resonance

Image Resonance asks:

* Does the participant recognize their testimony?
* Does the image feel faithful?
* Does the image preserve what remained?
* Does the image resonate with the witness?

Image Resonance precedes
Image Validation.

Documentation:

* image-resonance.md

## Resonance as Feedback

Participant responses provide
resonance information.

Examples:

* That's it.
* Close.
* Something feels wrong.
* That's not them.
* That feels strange.
* That feels unpleasant.

These responses should not be dismissed.

They provide information
about resonance.

## Resonance Before Correction

When resonance is low:

do not immediately correct.

First observe.

Ask:

* What feels wrong?
* What feels missing?
* What feels unfamiliar?
* What feels unlike the testimony?

Pattern Recognition may reveal
the source of non-resonance.

## Resonance and Fidelity

Resonance does not replace fidelity.

Fidelity remains primary.

However:

a failure of resonance may indicate
a failure of fidelity.

Resonance is therefore
a useful quality signal.

## One-Line Principle

Before validating correctness,
observe resonance.
