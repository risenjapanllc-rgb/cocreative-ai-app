# Perspective Selection

## Purpose

Perspective Selection determines
the correct camera perspective
required to preserve testimony fidelity.

It does not create scenes.

It does not create image prompts.

It only determines viewpoint.

---

## Core Principle

Protect witness perspective.

Do not replace witness perspective
with a cinematic camera.

---

## Input

Perspective Selection receives:

* Visual Extraction

---

## Output

Perspective Type

Perspective Rules

---

## Witness Fidelity

If testimony is first-person,

preserve first-person.

Do not convert to:

* third person
* observer camera
* cinematic camera
* over-the-shoulder camera

unless explicitly stated.

---

## Forbidden Inventions

Do not invent:

* camera positions
* observers
* spectators
* narrators

unless present in testimony.

---

## Failure Examples

Witness:

"I was in the tatami."

FAIL:

camera behind witness

---

Witness:

"I saw grandmother."

FAIL:

camera watching both people
from outside the scene

---

## Identity Preservation

Do not alter witness identity.

Witness:

adult woman

FAIL:

child
young girl
teenager

---

## Testimony Example

Witness:

* upper body emerging from tatami
* grandmother enters room
* grandmother calls "Yoko!"
* grandmother approaches

Perspective:

first person

Reason:

The witness experiences
the grandmother directly.

---

## Output Example

Perspective Type:

First Person

Rules:

* witness viewpoint
* no third person camera
* no over-the-shoulder framing
* no observer camera

---

## One-Line Principle

Protect the witness viewpoint.
Do not protect cinematic composition.
