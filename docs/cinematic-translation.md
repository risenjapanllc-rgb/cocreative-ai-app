# cinematic-translation.md

## Purpose

Cinematic Translation transforms
faithful testimony preservation
into faithful visual presentation.

It does not change testimony.

It does not add meaning.

It does not perform recognition.

Its purpose is to select
the visual presentation that best preserves
the lived experience of the testimony.

---

## Position

Testimony
↓
Visual Extraction
↓
Visual Extraction Fidelity
↓
Cinematic Translation
↓
Image Prompt
↓
Image Generation

---

## Core Principle

Preserve the experience.

Not merely the objects.

---

## What Cinematic Translation Does

Cinematic Translation may determine:

* scene count
* moment selection
* viewpoint
* distance
* framing
* composition
* focus
* light

These choices exist to preserve testimony.

They must not replace testimony.

---

## What Cinematic Translation Does Not Do

Do not add:

* symbolism
* theology
* psychology
* recognition
* inferred meaning
* invented events

---

## Scene Count Decision

Before selecting a viewpoint,
determine whether the testimony
can be faithfully represented
in a single image.

Ask:

* Is a single image sufficient?
* Would important testimony be lost?
* Are multiple scenes required?

Multiple scenes may be used
when necessary to preserve fidelity.

Scene count should be the minimum
required to preserve the experience.

---

## Moment Selection

Before selecting framing
or perspective,
identify the decisive moment.

Ask:

* What is the decisive moment?
* Which single moment best represents this scene?

Different moments may require
different visual treatment.

The decisive moment should be selected
before framing decisions are made.

---

## Witness-Centered Framing

Ask:

* What is the center of the experience?
* What must be visible?
* What must not be lost?

Framing should support
the witness experience.

Framing should not prioritize
visual attractiveness over fidelity.

---

## Perspective Selection

Perspective Fidelity does not require
a literal first-person camera.

The goal is not camera accuracy.

The goal is experience fidelity.

Possible viewpoints may include:

* first-person
* shoulder view
* close witness position
* slightly behind the witness
* wider contextual framing

Select the viewpoint
that best preserves
the lived experience.

---

## One-Line Principle

Act like a faithful film director.

Preserve the experience,
not merely the scene.
